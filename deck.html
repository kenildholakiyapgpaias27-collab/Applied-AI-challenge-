✦ Melanin-Aware Safety Rules

Why Skin Tone Dictates Product Safety

In dermatological science, skin tone isn't just cosmetic—it is a critical safety constraint. Darker skin complexions have hyper-reactive melanocytes that require fundamentally different active ingredient rules.

SAFETY CONSTRAINT 01

Post-Inflammatory Hyperpigmentation (PIH)

On melanocompetent skin (Monk 5–10), aggressive chemical peels or unbuffered high-strength acids trigger trauma in the basal melanocytes, causing long-lasting dark spots that take up to a year to fade.

AAINA Rule: Hard ceiling on unbuffered Glycolic Acid (≤7%) and Salicylic Acid (≤2%). Automatically favors gentle Niacinamide and Azelaic Acid.
SAFETY CONSTRAINT 02

Sunscreen White-Cast Penalty

Pure physical sunscreens (Zinc Oxide and Titanium Dioxide) scatter visible light. On deeper complexions, this leaves an unsightly purple-gray chalky film, causing users to skip daily sunscreen.

AAINA Rule: Automatically penalizes pure mineral zinc formulations on Monk 6–10 and recommends invisible hybrid or photostable chemical UV filters.

Real-Time Climate & City Tuning

Your skin behaves differently in humid Mumbai than in dry winter Delhi. AAINA adapts product textures and UV filters based on real-time climate telemetry.

High Humidity (Coastal Cities: Mumbai, Chennai)
